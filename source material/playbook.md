Early Adoption Playbook

(Inspired by Ad Hoc, adapted for GovCon & Complex Systems)

WHY THE AD HOC PLAYBOOK WORKS (AND WHY IT’S A GOOD MODEL)

The Ad Hoc Playbook succeeds because it is:

Normative, not aspirational

Concrete, not theoretical

Behavior-focused, not tool-focused

Consumable in small pieces

Grounded in lived constraints

Most importantly, it answers a psychological question first:

“What kind of organization am I stepping into?”

FOE must do the same — but with a different threat model:

Higher regulatory burden

Longer program lifetimes

Greater consequences of failure

Increasing use of automation and agents

FOE PLAYBOOK DESIGN PRINCIPLES

(Directly borrowed + evolved from Ad Hoc)

1. Short Sections, Strong Claims

No white papers

No encyclopedias

Each page answers one anxiety

2. Behavior Over Belief

FOE is not “what we value”

FOE is “how we behave when things are unclear”

3. Reality-First

Describe how work actually happens

Acknowledge constraints

Name tradeoffs explicitly

4. Legibility as a Moral Good

If you can’t explain it, you can’t scale it

If you can’t show it, you can’t defend it

THE FOE PLAYBOOK (EARLY-STAGE EDITION)

This is the minimum viable FOE playbook — intentionally small, intentionally sharp.

1. How We Think About Speed

Audience: Executives · Government · Engineers

Core Claim

Speed is not the enemy.
Surprise is.

What This Means in Practice

We don’t optimize for output.

We optimize for observable progress.

Work should become clearer as it moves forward, not more mysterious.

Why This Exists (Psychological)

People fear speed when they lose visibility.
We remove the fear by increasing legibility.

2. How We Manage Risk

Audience: Executives · Government

Core Claim

Risk doesn’t disappear when you slow down.
It just hides longer.

Our Approach

Detect risk early

Contain it locally

Make it explainable

What You’ll See

Clear change narratives

Evidence tied to decisions

Fewer “surprises at the end”

This mirrors Ad Hoc’s clarity about constraints — but translated into risk economics.

3. How Work Moves Through the System

Audience: Engineers · Program Managers

Core Claim

If work can’t be traced, it can’t be trusted.

What We Do

Every change has intent

Every change produces evidence

Every change can be explained after the fact

What This Is Not

Not extra paperwork

Not approval theater

Not micromanagement

This is FOE’s Understanding optimization, expressed behaviorally.

4. How We Learn (Without Blame)

Audience: Engineers · Leadership

Core Claim

Learning requires feedback before damage becomes irreversible.

Our Default Behaviors

Small changes

Early signals

Clear rollback paths

What This Enables

Faster iteration

Less fear

Fewer heroic recoveries

This directly implements Fast Feedback without calling it that.

5. How We Make Decisions Under Uncertainty

Audience: Executives · Engineers

Core Claim

Confidence comes from knowing what will happen if we’re wrong.

What We Optimize For

Reversible decisions

Visible consequences

Bounded downside

What This Replaces

Over-analysis

Escalation-by-default

Decision paralysis

This is Confidence, defined psychologically — not emotionally.

6. What We Expect From Leaders

Audience: Executives · Gov PMs

Borrowing Ad Hoc’s clarity about leadership expectations.

We Expect Leaders To:

Ask for evidence, not assurances

Protect teams from surprise deadlines

Reward clarity over heroics

We Do Not Expect Leaders To:

Be technical experts

Approve every decision

Guess when things are “safe”

7. What We Expect From Engineers

Audience: Engineers

We Expect Engineers To:

Make their work legible

Surface risk early

Prefer boring, repeatable solutions

We Do Not Expect Engineers To:

Memorize systems

Be heroes

Carry invisible risk alone

8. How We Talk to Government Partners

Audience: External

This is critical and often missing.

Our Default Posture

Calm. Clear. Evidence-first.

What We Show

How change is controlled

How risk is detected

How learning happens continuously

What We Avoid

Jargon

Hype

Claims without receipts

This mirrors Ad Hoc’s emphasis on trust through transparency — adapted for audit culture.

Understanding	Sections 1, 3, 6, 7

Confidence	Sections 2, 5, 8

The playbook never names the optimizations explicitly — it demonstrates them.
