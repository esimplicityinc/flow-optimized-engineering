# Collaboration Mode

**Pillar:** Interaction Modes

Collaboration is a team interaction mode where two teams work closely together for a defined period, typically to discover new things—APIs, practices, technologies, or approaches. It's high-bandwidth, high-cost communication that should be time-boxed.

## Definition

> "Collaboration: two teams work closely together for a defined period of time to discover new things (APIs, practices, technologies, etc.)."
>
> — Skelton & Pais, Team Topologies

## Why It Matters

Collaboration mode is essential for discovery and innovation:

- **High bandwidth** - Rich communication enables rapid learning and problem-solving
- **Discovery** - Best for exploring unknown territory together
- **Boundary definition** - Helps teams find the right API boundaries
- **Innovation** - New ideas emerge from close collaboration
- **Trust building** - Teams develop stronger working relationships

However, collaboration is expensive and should be used deliberately.

## FOE Framing

**Primary: Optimize for Understanding**

Collaboration mode maximizes understanding through high-bandwidth communication. When teams work closely together, knowledge transfers rapidly. Both teams develop shared mental models of the problem space and solution.

**Secondary: Fast Feedback Loops**

During collaboration, feedback is immediate. Teams learn quickly what works and what doesn't. Misunderstandings are caught and corrected in real-time.

## When to Use

- **New integrations** - Discovering how two systems should work together
- **Technology adoption** - Learning new technologies together
- **API design** - Finding the right boundaries and contracts
- **Problem exploration** - Understanding complex problems jointly
- **Innovation projects** - Creating something genuinely new

## How It Works

### Characteristics of Effective Collaboration

1. **Time-boxed** - Clear start and end dates (weeks to months, not years)
2. **Explicit goals** - Both teams know what they're discovering
3. **Joint ownership** - Both teams contribute and take responsibility
4. **High communication** - Frequent sync, shared channels, co-location (physical or virtual)
5. **Transition plan** - Clear path to another interaction mode when done

### Collaboration Lifecycle

| Phase | Activities |
|-------|------------|
| **Kick-off** | Define goals, success criteria, and timeline |
| **Deep collaboration** | Joint working sessions, shared ownership |
| **Discovery** | Find the right boundaries, APIs, practices |
| **Documentation** | Capture learnings, define contracts |
| **Transition** | Move to X-as-a-Service or Facilitating mode |

### Collaboration Anti-Patterns

- **Indefinite collaboration** - No end date, becomes permanent coupling
- **Asymmetric effort** - One team does all the work
- **No clear goals** - Collaborating without knowing what to discover
- **Avoiding difficult conversations** - Using "collaboration" to delay decisions
- **Too many collaborations** - Teams spread too thin

## Relationship to Other Methods

- **X-as-a-Service Mode** - Common next step after collaboration defines APIs
- **Facilitating Mode** - Alternative when one team is teaching another
- **Enabling Team** - Often uses collaboration during intensive skill transfer
- **Stream-Aligned Team** - May collaborate with platform or other stream-aligned teams
- **Team Cognitive Load** - Collaboration is cognitively expensive

## When NOT to Use

Avoid collaboration mode when:

- Clear APIs already exist (use X-as-a-Service)
- One team is teaching another (use Facilitating)
- The relationship should be ongoing service consumption
- Neither team has capacity for high-bandwidth communication
- Goals can be achieved through lower-cost interaction
