# Level 2: Repeatable

> **Theme: "We can change things safely"**

At Level 2, teams have established patterns that work. Changes follow known paths. Evidence is generated automatically. This is where structured delivery discipline starts to appear.

:::tip Phase 3 Connection
Level 2 is where Phase 3 practices (Structured Delivery with Confidence) begin to take hold. The predictability you see here comes from intentional process design.
:::

## What Changed

- Standardized delivery paths exist for common work types
- Change patterns are repeatable—teams know what to expect
- Evidence is generated automatically, not assembled manually
- Rollback procedures are defined and tested
- New team members can follow established patterns

## Executive Experience

- Routine delivery is predictable
- Risk is visible earlier in the process
- Confidence is justified for familiar change types
- Status is available without special effort
- Surprises still happen, but they're less frequent

## Engineering Reality

- Common changes follow well-understood paths
- CI/CD pipelines catch problems before deployment
- Code review processes are consistent
- Testing is automated for known scenarios
- Documentation is generated as part of the process

## Government View

- Evidence is available without special preparation
- Compliance can be demonstrated through system outputs
- Audit trails are complete for standard processes
- Risk is managed through repeatable controls
- Reviews focus on exceptions rather than baseline proof

## Optimization Breakdown

| Optimization | State | Characteristics |
|--------------|-------|-----------------|
| **Understanding** | Shared, documented, current | Knowledge is captured in systems, not just people. Documentation is updated as part of work. Understanding is accessible to anyone. |
| **Fast Feedback** | Early and actionable | Problems surface during development, not after. Feedback loops are short enough to be useful. Teams can act on what they learn. |
| **Confidence** | High for routine change | Teams trust the process for familiar work. Decisions are made without escalation. Risk is managed, not avoided. |

## Key Smell

> "We've done this before, and we know how it behaves."

When teams can predict outcomes based on experience, and that experience is encoded in process, you've achieved repeatability. The system has memory.

:::info What This Looks Like
- Deployment checklists that are actually followed
- Automated test suites that catch regressions
- Change management processes that add value
- Metrics that teams actually use for decisions
- Runbooks that work when you need them
:::

## The Limits of Level 2

Level 2 is stable and reliable, but it has boundaries:

- Novel changes still create uncertainty
- Process can become rigid if not maintained
- Feedback is early but not continuous
- Confidence depends on staying within known patterns

Moving to Level 3 requires the system to start teaching you, not just following your instructions.

## Movement Indicators

You're moving beyond Level 2 when:
- Feedback loops tighten to near-continuous
- Evidence becomes a byproduct, not an output
- Novel changes become less scary because the system helps
- Teams trust the system, not just the process
