# What We Expect From Leaders

<span class="badge badge--primary">Executives</span> <span class="badge badge--info">Government PMs</span>

## We Expect Leaders To

### Ask for evidence, not assurances

- "Show me" beats "trust me"
- Dashboards over status meetings
- Receipts over promises

### Protect teams from surprise deadlines

- Commitments made without team input aren't commitments
- Buffer time is not laziness—it's risk management
- Urgency should be explained, not just asserted

### Reward clarity over heroics

- Celebrate problems surfaced early
- Promote people who make work legible
- Treat "I don't know yet" as valid status

## We Do NOT Expect Leaders To

### Be technical experts

You don't need to understand the code. You need to understand whether the team can explain what they're doing and why.

### Approve every decision

Your job is to set constraints and priorities, not to be a bottleneck. If you're approving routine decisions, something is broken.

### Guess when things are "safe"

That's what evidence is for. If you're guessing, ask for better signals.

## What Good Leadership Looks Like Here

| Instead of... | Try... |
|---------------|--------|
| "Is this going to work?" | "What will tell us if it's not working?" |
| "When will it be done?" | "What's blocking progress right now?" |
| "Why didn't anyone tell me?" | "How do we surface this earlier next time?" |
| "Who's responsible?" | "What did we learn?" |

## The Leader's Job

Create conditions where:

- Truth travels fast
- Problems are cheap to surface
- Good work is visible
- Risk is discussable
