# The Triad

Doctrine is cheap.

Execution is expensive.

FOE works only when **people, process, and technology** change together.

Treat them like independent initiatives and you’ll get the worst kind of failure: the kind that costs money *and* morale.

## The three dimensions

1. **People** — skills, identity, incentives, ownership.
2. **Process** — how work moves, how checks happen, how learning sticks.
3. **Technology** — the substrate that makes fast feedback and guardrails real.

They’re a system.

## The three classic failure modes

### Technology without process

You “have Kubernetes.”

You still deploy like it’s 2012.

Tools become shelfware.

### Process without people

You mandate reviews, checklists, and gates.

People comply performatively.

You get theater.

### People without technology

You hire smart, motivated folks.

Then you trap them in broken tooling and manual toil.

You get burnout.

## The rule

If you want sustained speed, you must design the triad **as one change**.

Start with a specific flow problem.

Map the triad.

Change all three together.

Measure outcomes.
